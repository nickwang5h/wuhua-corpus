It can be easy to notice when someone else is tired or distracted. Noticing the same thing in ourselves can take a little longer.

Chinese has a short expression for that inward kind of knowing:

<Phrase chinese="自知" pinyin="zì zhī" gloss="self-knowledge; knowing yourself" />

自 points toward oneself, and 知 means “to know”. You may meet 自知 in written reflections. In everyday conversation, a fuller sentence often feels more natural.

## Start with something small

<Phrase chinese="我想了解自己。" pinyin="wǒ xiǎng liǎo jiě zì jǐ" gloss="I want to understand myself." />

This modern sentence does not ask you to explain your whole personality. It might begin with a smaller observation: you make rushed decisions when you are hungry, or you understand an idea better after talking it through.

Knowing that does not make the habit disappear. It gives you a chance to respond differently next time.

## Looking outward, looking inward

The *Daodejing* places two kinds of understanding beside one another:

<ClassicQuote source="main" />

One way to read this is: understanding others is wisdom; understanding yourself is clarity. The word 明 carries the idea of brightness or clear seeing. Here it helps describe self-understanding, not knowing every answer.

The next lines make a similar comparison between overcoming others and overcoming oneself. The chapter is drawing our attention inward without saying that understanding other people has no value.

For today, you could hold on to just one question: what have I noticed about myself recently? A small, specific answer is enough. You do not need a finished theory of who you are.
