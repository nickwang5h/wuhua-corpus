What makes someone rich? A bigger house, more time, or the feeling that there is nothing else they need right now?

One useful Chinese expression is:

<Phrase chinese="知足" pinyin="zhī zú" gloss="to be content with what is enough" />

知 means “to know”. 足 means “enough” here. Put them together and you get an idea that is less about counting possessions and more about recognising sufficiency.

## A small, ordinary moment

Imagine finishing a simple meal with a friend. You could order more, but you are comfortable, the conversation is good, and you are happy to stay where you are.

<Phrase chinese="我很知足。" pinyin="wǒ hěn zhī zú" gloss="I am quite content with what I have." />

This is a modern example, not a quotation from a classic. 我 means “I”; 很 often connects a person with a description, without necessarily putting a strong emphasis on “very”.

Being content does not mean that everyone already has what they need. Food, shelter and safety matter. The expression gives us a way to talk about the feeling of enough, rather than a rule about what other people should accept.

## An old way of asking the question

Chapter 33 of the *Daodejing* puts the idea very briefly:

<ClassicQuote source="main" />

A person who knows enough is rich. Here, “rich” becomes a way to describe contentment. The chapter also compares knowing others with knowing yourself: it keeps turning attention from what we can measure outside to what we can notice within.

You do not have to stop wanting things to try this thought. The next time something feels sufficient, pause long enough to notice it. What makes that moment enough?
