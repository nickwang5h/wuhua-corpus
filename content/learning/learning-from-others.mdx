Think of something useful you picked up from another person without a formal lesson. Perhaps a friend showed you how to ask a better question, or a colleague stayed calm when a plan changed.

In Chinese, a teacher is:

<Phrase chinese="老师" pinyin="lǎo shī" gloss="teacher" />

You can use this word for someone whose job is teaching. But the idea of learning from other people reaches beyond that role.

## Learning can happen beside you

<Phrase chinese="我向你学习。" pinyin="wǒ xiàng nǐ xué xí" gloss="I learn from you." />

This modern sentence points toward a person as a source of learning. It can express respect: there is something in what you do that I would like to understand or practise myself.

It does not mean agreeing with everything that person says. You might admire someone's patience while choosing a different approach to another part of life.

## A teacher on the walk

A passage in the *Analects* imagines being in the company of other people:

<ClassicQuote source="main" />

The thought is that, walking with others, I can be sure to find someone to learn from. 師, the older written form of 师, carries the idea of a teacher. 行 is read *xíng* here and refers to walking or going along.

The rest of the passage explains the distinction: follow what is good, and use what is not good to examine and correct yourself. Learning is an attentive choice, not copying everything someone does.

The next time a person does something you appreciate, try naming exactly what you noticed. “They are impressive” is one thought. “They gave someone time to finish speaking” is something you can try yourself.
